\section{CONCLUSION}

This work confirmed the technical feasibility of neuromorphic prediction of water quality in
Lago do Serpa by combining multimodal modeling, spatial analysis, and embedded deployment. In
terms of performance, the SNN achieved competitive and stable results, outperforming the MLP in
the multi-seed benchmark and remaining close to classical baselines. In the evaluated dataset,
Random Forest and LSTM achieved lower global mean absolute error, while the SNN remained a robust
alternative for neuromorphic edge inference. In addition, loss-weight tuning produced a specific
improvement in turbidity with a moderate trade-off in macro performance.

On the application axis, the model was exported to TensorFlow Lite and integrated into an offline
mobile app, enabling local inference of pH, OD, and turbidity, composite pollution index
calculation, and geospatial visualization with an offline map (MBTiles). This result advances
the work beyond a purely algorithmic proof of concept by demonstrating a full pipeline of data,
training, embedded inference, and field decision support.

In neuromorphic design, \textit{rate coding} was also coherent with the edge scenario, as it
allows explicit control of the balance between temporal representation quality and mobile
inference latency via the parameter $T$. As limitations, the study still relies on a local and
reduced sample base, which restricts generalization to other water bodies and seasonal regimes;
synthetic data augmentation does not replace real-world collection, and model differences may
vary with new field campaigns. Practical validation was carried out on Android, Raspberry Pi,
and ESP32, but extrapolation to other microcontrollers and different energy profiles requires
additional measurements and broader field campaigns. As future continuity, it is recommended to
expand data collection, validate the model across multiple environmental scenarios, and
systematically measure latency, energy consumption, and operational robustness on different edge
devices. It is also relevant to refine the calibration of the composite pollution index with
support from environmental specialists and to test field model-update strategies under different
hydrological regimes.

As an additional contribution, ESP32 validation showed that multimodal inference is feasible on
microcontrollers, with millisecond-level latency and absolute errors compatible with the mobile
scenario. The energy comparison among ESP32, Raspberry Pi, and smartphones revealed clear
trade-offs between throughput and energy per inference, reinforcing the importance of selecting
platform and sampling regime according to operational context. Thus, the core differentiator of
this work is not only the use of SNNs, but the delivery of a complete and measurable offline
inference pipeline: data \(\rightarrow\) model \(\rightarrow\) app \(\rightarrow\) map \(\rightarrow\) energy.
