\section{DISCUSSION}

The results indicate that the proposed neuromorphic approach is viable for predicting
physicochemical water parameters in the Amazonian context, with consistent performance after
hyperparameter tuning and \textit{early stopping} regularization. In the multimodal scenario
without information leakage (i.e., target variables are not used as inputs), prediction
difficulty increases, especially for turbidity, which makes the evaluation more realistic for
field use.

In the 10-seed benchmark, Random Forest and LSTM achieved lower global mean errors, while the
SNN remained competitive and clearly more robust than the MLP. Statistical tests reinforce
this reading: there was no evidence of statistical superiority of the SNN over Random Forest
and LSTM at $\alpha=0{,}05$, but there was a significant advantage of the SNN over MLP.
Therefore, the results support SNN as a viable edge alternative, even if it is not dominant
in absolute error on the current dataset. Spatially, the pH, OD, and turbidity maps reveal
coherent gradients and clusters, reinforcing the consistency of field data and suggesting
that input variables carry relevant information for prediction. The correlations between
temperature, apparent color, and turbidity observed in the exploratory analysis align with
expected physicochemical behavior reported for lentic systems in the water-quality literature,
particularly regarding the relevance of thermal regime and oxygen dynamics, and the recurring
use of physicochemical predictors in data-driven monitoring studies
\cite{jane2021widespread,yan2024}; moreover, the adoption of pH, dissolved oxygen, and
turbidity as core response variables is consistent with regulatory and monitoring practice in
Brazilian inland waters \cite{conama3572005}, providing additional support for the proposed
model and for the environmental interpretability of the predicted outputs without claiming
site-specific causality beyond the observed dataset.
Although Random Forest achieved lower macro error on this dataset, it does not provide
event-driven temporal processing or a direct path to neuromorphic deployment, which keeps the
SNN relevant for low-power adaptive edge scenarios where hardware compatibility and temporal
sparse computation are part of the design objective rather than only predictive accuracy.

Beyond predictive performance, model execution on the Raspberry Pi 4B achieved sub-millisecond
latency per sample, reinforcing the feasibility of local inference on low-power hardware
compatible with remote and offline scenarios. Validation on the ESP32 extends this result to
microcontrollers: despite lower throughput, the benchmark indicated millisecond-level latency
and consistent absolute error, demonstrating that the multimodal model can operate autonomously
on highly constrained devices.

From a practical standpoint, differences among models suggest that the choice depends on the
operational objective. Random Forest and LSTM tend to minimize mean error, while the SNN offers
a robust alternative when local inference with lower computational cost and potential energy
efficiency is prioritized. The more unstable behavior of the MLP confirms that dense classical
models are sensitive on small datasets and can vary significantly across seeds. Additionally,
systematic difficulty in turbidity indicates that this parameter requires additional
information (e.g., hydrodynamic or seasonal variables) to improve generalization.
Although the original field dataset comprises 60 samples, synthetic augmentation and repeated
seed-based splits were used to reduce variance and sensitivity to overfitting; however, these
strategies do not replace broader field campaigns and external validation across additional
hydrological contexts.

In energy terms, the comparison among ESP32, Raspberry Pi, and Android devices shows that
incremental energy per inference varies significantly with hardware and available throughput.
The ESP32 yielded higher energy per inference but remains suitable for low-rate sampling
applications, while Raspberry Pi and smartphones offer better per-inference efficiency in
scenarios requiring higher prediction volumes. These results confirm that platform choice
should consider not only accuracy, but also sampling regime and energy constraints in the
deployment environment.

Another important consideration is that temporal encoding via \textit{rate coding} implies a
trade-off between temporal fidelity and computational cost: larger $T$ values can improve
representation quality, but increase inference latency and energy use. This suggests that, in
real-world deployments, $T$ should be calibrated according to operational context and energy
constraints of the edge device.
