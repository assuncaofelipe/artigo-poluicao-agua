\section{RELATED WORK}

\subsection{Deep learning, hybrid models, and reliability in water quality}

Water quality modeling has evolved from classical approaches to deep neural networks, with
recurrent models standing out in environmental time series. \citeonline{pyo2023} report consistent
gains with LSTM in inland waters, while \citeonline{qi2020} and \citeonline{liu2019} reinforce the
effectiveness of recurrent architectures and IoT for predicting physicochemical parameters. Recent
reviews confirm the dominance of deep learning for nonlinear hydrological patterns
\cite{yan2024,pan2025}. In parallel, hybrid approaches seek robustness under noise;
\citeonline{wu2022} integrate ANN, Wavelet, and LSTM, and comparative studies evaluate multiple
algorithms for water-quality parameters \cite{ahmed2019,kalaivanan2022,aladhaileh2021}. These results
show accuracy gains but increase preprocessing and inference cost when local execution on constrained
hardware is required. Instrument reliability is also critical: \citeonline{elshafeiy2023} address
real-time anomaly detection, while \citeonline{lamrini2024} evaluate outliers in smart sensing; IoT
system reviews highlight that performance depends on data architecture, calibration, and sensor-network
robustness \cite{zulkifli2022,miller2025}.

\subsection{Edge AI, neuromorphic computing, and SNN fundamentals}

Edge AI has been proposed to reduce latency and cloud dependence in water monitoring
\cite{rahu2023,lowe2022}. Applied studies in sensor networks and remote monitoring show operational
feasibility but also highlight hardware, energy, and connectivity limits \cite{rosero2020,alprol2024}.
In the Amazon, these constraints motivate local inference architectures with lower computational cost.
Strategic literature emphasizes that neuromorphic edge architectures can reduce energy consumption
and improve resilience in low-infrastructure areas \cite{aral2024}; recent results in embedded
Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) also report energy gains with neuromorphic hardware
\cite{azadi2025}, while ESP32-based solutions demonstrate real-time monitoring feasibility
\cite{alshareeda2025}. To mitigate the energy cost of conventional ANN inference, neuromorphic
computing relies on event-driven processing. In SNNs, information is encoded as asynchronous spikes,
reducing computation when inputs are stable. SNN accelerators with compute-in-memory report both
performance and energy efficiency gains \cite{huijbregts2024}, and recent reviews consolidate
modeling, training, and latency/energy metrics \cite{aribe2025future}. Among bio-inspired models,
\citeonline{chadalavada2025} propose Gated Liquid Neural Networks for water-quality indices, but still
in continuous regimes; SNNs exploit event-driven inference and are potentially better aligned with
edge constraints.

Among models that describe spiking neuron dynamics, Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) offers a balance
between biological plausibility and computational efficiency. The membrane potential $V(t)$ follows
the linear differential equation:

$$\tau_m \frac{dV(t)}{dt} = -(V(t) - V_{rest}) + R_m I(t)$$

Here, $\tau_m$ is the membrane time constant, $V_{rest}$ the resting potential, $R_m$ the membrane
resistance, and $I(t)$ the synaptic input current. A spike is emitted when $V(t)$ crosses a threshold
$V_{th}$, and the potential is reset to $V_{reset}$.

For digital implementations (e.g., microcontrollers), the continuous model is discretized as:

$$V[t+1] = V[t] + \frac{1}{\tau_m} (-(V[t] - V_{rest}) + I[t]) \Delta t$$

This formulation highlights a key advantage for edge deployment: when inputs are stable
($I[t] \approx 0$), the system evolves with low activity, favoring energy efficiency on constrained
devices.

\subsection{Local context and positioning of this work}

In the Lago do Serpa context, local studies on territory and environment reinforce the social and
ecological relevance of continuous monitoring \cite{nogueira2022}. The experiment was conducted in
the Amazon region, specifically at Lago do Serpa (Itacoatiara, AM), a lentic environment embedded in
complex hydrological dynamics and seasonal variability. Data collection relied on in-situ
measurements and georeferenced sampling points distributed across the lake to capture spatial
gradients and local heterogeneity in physicochemical parameters (Figure~\ref{fig:mapa_pontos}). The
sampling design emphasizes coverage of the lake surface rather than dense temporal repetition at a
single location, which is consistent with the goal of producing spatially interpretable maps and
identifying potential micro-zones of degradation. This spatial emphasis is critical in Amazonian
settings where access is logistically constrained and where localized inputs (e.g., sediment
resuspension, small inflows, or human activity near the margins) can alter water quality within short
distances. Classic ANN-based water-quality forecasting demonstrated modeling potential in different
scenarios \cite{palani2008}, but without a focus on neuromorphic edge inference. LSTM and remote
sensing studies report accuracy gains and thematic maps in large lakes \cite{qi2020}, while IoT
frameworks and wireless sensor networks prioritize acquisition and prediction but typically depend
on continuous connectivity \cite{rahu2023,rosero2020}. Bio-inspired models such as Liquid Neural
Networks achieve good results for water-quality indices but operate in continuous regimes and do not
fully exploit event-driven inference \cite{chadalavada2025}. Strategic Edge AI studies highlight the
potential of SNNs for rural environments, yet lack complete experimental implementation
\cite{aral2024}. This work positions itself by integrating (i) multimodal prediction of pH,
dissolved oxygen, and turbidity; (ii) spatial analysis and pollution maps; and (iii) offline
deployment on edge devices with latency and energy benchmarking. This combination remains
underexplored and addresses practical gaps for Amazonian field operations with limited connectivity
and energy.
