\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Water quality monitoring in the Amazon basin is a logistical challenge due to vast distances and
limited infrastructure. This paper evaluates the feasibility of deploying Spiking Neural Networks
(SNNs) at the edge to predict water-quality parameters with offline inference. Using data collected
in Lago do Serpa (Itacoatiara, AM), we model pH, turbidity, and dissolved oxygen and assess a
multimodal SNN pipeline that includes preprocessing, hyperparameter search, spatial mapping, and
deployment on mobile and embedded devices. Results show competitive predictive performance and
practical feasibility for offline operation. The main contribution is not only the SNN model itself,
but the end-to-end pipeline with empirical latency and energy measurements on edge hardware.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Index Terms—} Spiking Neural Networks, edge AI, water quality, offline inference, neuromorphic computing, environmental monitoring.
