\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This work investigates neuromorphic edge inference for water-quality monitoring in Lago do Serpa
(Itacoatiara, AM) using a spiking neural network (SNN) deployed on low-cost devices. The proposed
pipeline operates directly on field measurements (coordinates and physicochemical inputs) to jointly
predict pH, dissolved oxygen, and turbidity without relying on cloud connectivity. A small,
georeferenced dataset was expanded via light Gaussian augmentation to stabilize training, and the
SNN was optimized through a systematic grid search over time steps, learning rate, threshold, decay,
and hidden-layer size. The final model adopts a leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) hidden layer and
achieves competitive accuracy in the multimodal setting, while enabling fully offline inference. The
study further reports latency and energy benchmarks on Raspberry Pi, Android devices, and ESP32,
including INA219-based power telemetry and incremental energy estimates under idle/load conditions in
MicroPython. Results demonstrate that neuromorphic inference is feasible on constrained hardware,
with practical throughput for field use and measurable energy trade-offs across platforms, supporting
autonomous monitoring where connectivity and power are limited.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Index Terms—} Spiking Neural Networks, edge AI, water quality, offline inference, neuromorphic computing, environmental monitoring.
