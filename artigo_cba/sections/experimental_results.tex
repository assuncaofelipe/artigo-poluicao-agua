\subsection{Prediction Performance}
Table~\ref{tab:pred_perf} summarizes macro performance in a multi-seed benchmark ($n=10$), reporting mean and standard deviation. Random Forest and LSTM obtained the lowest average errors, while the proposed SNN remained competitive and substantially more stable than the MLP baseline.

\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Model & MAE macro & RMSE macro & $R^2$ macro \\
\midrule
Random Forest & $\mathbf{0.409 \pm 0.062}$ & $0.557 \pm 0.086$ & $\mathbf{0.335 \pm 0.131}$ \\
LSTM & $0.428 \pm 0.059$ & $\mathbf{0.548 \pm 0.073}$ & $0.313 \pm 0.139$ \\
SNN (proposed) & $0.447 \pm 0.064$ & $0.566 \pm 0.072$ & $0.291 \pm 0.146$ \\

Although Random Forest achieved the lowest MAE, the SNN demonstrated competitive predictive performance while enabling deployment on low-power edge devices, which is essential for remote environmental monitoring scenarios. The ranking indicates that the SNN is not the lowest-error model in absolute terms, but it remains in a competitive range with materially better robustness than MLP in this small-data regime.

{\sloppy\rev{To quantify uncertainty, 95\% confidence intervals (t-distribution, $n=10$ seeds) were computed for all macro metrics. For MAE macro: SNN $[0.399, 0.495]$, Random Forest $[0.362, 0.456]$, LSTM $[0.384, 0.472]$, and MLP $[0.636, 0.926]$; for $R^2$ macro: SNN $[0.181, 0.401]$, Random Forest $[0.237, 0.434]$, LSTM $[0.209, 0.418]$, and MLP $[-9.396, -1.524]$. The intervals of SNN, Random Forest, and LSTM overlap substantially, whereas MLP is clearly separated.}\par}

\rev{Complementing the sign test reported in the original submission, exact Wilcoxon signed-rank tests over the ten paired seeds show no significant difference between SNN and Random Forest (MAE $p=0.064$; RMSE $p=0.846$; $R^2$ $p=0.922$) or between SNN and LSTM (MAE $p=0.105$; RMSE $p=0.131$; $R^2$ $p=0.232$), while SNN significantly outperforms MLP on all metrics ($p=0.002$). A Friedman test across the four models is significant (MAE: $\chi^2(3)=19.56$, $p<0.001$), with mean ranks of 1.7 (Random Forest), 1.9 (LSTM), 2.4 (SNN), and 4.0 (MLP), confirming that the global difference is driven by MLP rather than by separation among the three leading models.}

\subsection{Augmentation Ablation}
To quantify augmentation impact, we compared SNN training with no augmentation (factor 1) and augmentation $\times 10$ (Gaussian noise, $\sigma=0.01$) under the same protocol across five seeds (7, 13, 21, 42, 84). Augmentation reduced macro MAE from $0.496 \pm 0.060$ to $0.483 \pm 0.075$ (2.53\% reduction), with slight macro $R^2$ improvement ($0.254 \rightarrow 0.256$) and nearly unchanged macro RMSE ($0.615 \rightarrow 0.617$). The most relevant per-target gain was for turbidity MAE ($1.139 \rightarrow 1.102$), supporting augmentation as a practical stabilization strategy for this dataset size.
