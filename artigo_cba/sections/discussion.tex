\section{Discussion}

Results show a clear trade-off between predictive accuracy and deployment constraints. In the multi-seed benchmark, Random Forest and LSTM reached lower average macro errors, while the SNN remained competitive and clearly more stable than MLP. \rev{Wilcoxon and Friedman outcomes, together with the overlapping confidence intervals, indicate that Random Forest, LSTM, and the SNN are statistically indistinguishable on this dataset, which reinforces that the SNN should be interpreted as a robust edge-oriented alternative rather than a universal best-accuracy model.} From a methodological standpoint, the multimodal setup proved valuable because it keeps physically related targets in a single inference pipeline and simplifies downstream decision logic in field applications.

From the systems perspective, platform behavior is heterogeneous: Device B reached the highest throughput, Device C achieved the lowest incremental energy per inference, and ESP32 provided full offline feasibility at much lower processing rates. Since baseline platform power varies with architecture and operating-system behavior, energy per inference combined with throughput is a more actionable criterion than absolute watts alone. Augmentation ablation showed a modest but consistent macro-MAE improvement (2.53\%) and turbidity gain, indicating that controlled synthetic expansion helps stabilize training in this low-sample regime.

\subsection{Practical Deployment Guidance}
The combined results allow a simple deployment guideline for field teams: for dense temporal sampling, frequent map updates, or near-real-time dashboards, smartphone or SBC-class execution is preferable; for sparse periodic checks with strict hardware simplicity, ESP32 remains viable and operationally attractive despite higher mJ/inf values.

A second practical point is model-selection policy: in constrained field scenarios, selecting only by minimum MAE can be suboptimal. A better criterion is a multi-objective envelope combining acceptable prediction error, stable seed behavior, platform throughput, and incremental energy per inference, extensible with constraints such as battery budget and telemetry cadence.

\rev{The choice of the SNN over Random Forest deserves explicit justification, since Random Forest achieved the lowest mean MAE. First, the accuracy difference is not statistically significant: Wilcoxon tests and confidence intervals show full overlap between the two models. Second, the SNN offers properties that Random Forest does not: event-driven computation whose cost scales with spike activity, a single compact dense model that exports directly to ONNX/TFLite for all three platform classes, and forward compatibility with neuromorphic accelerators, where sparse spiking execution can reduce energy well below the CPU-based measurements reported here. Third, the temporal encoding parameter $T$ provides a tunable accuracy--energy knob that has no counterpart in tree ensembles. The SNN is therefore selected not as the lowest-error model, but as the statistically equivalent model with the best deployment envelope.}

\rev{Regarding Edge AI versus Cloud AI, the trade-off goes beyond raw compute. Cloud inference offers larger models and simpler updates, but in remote Amazonian deployments it adds radio energy that typically dominates the per-sample budget, latency and availability tied to intermittent links, recurring connectivity costs, and exposure of raw environmental data to third-party infrastructure. Fully offline edge inference inverts this profile: energy is bounded by local computation (measured here at $\mu$J-to-mJ scale per inference), monitoring continues during outages, and only aggregated results need eventual synchronization. A hybrid pattern --- local screening with opportunistic cloud consolidation --- is a natural evolution of the present pipeline.}

\subsection{Error Interpretation and Validity}
The predicted-vs-true plots indicate a clear target-dependent error profile: pH and OD follow the identity trend with lower dispersion, while turbidity exhibits wider spread. This behavior is coherent with freshwater heterogeneity and reinforces that turbidity should be interpreted with broader confidence margins in operational use. From an application perspective, this asymmetry supports a layered decision strategy: treat pH and OD as stronger short-term screening signals, and use turbidity primarily to flag candidate zones for additional sampling rather than as a standalone trigger. \rev{For dissolved oxygen specifically, the per-target analysis shows that no evaluated model achieves positive mean $R^2$, which points to a feature-set limitation rather than a modeling one: the current inputs carry little OD-related information ($|r| \leq 0.24$). Improving OD prediction will therefore require enriching the input modality --- e.g., conductivity, oxidation-reduction potential, chlorophyll, or diel temperature profiles --- rather than further model tuning.}

Threats to validity remain centered on sample size and regional representativeness. Although augmentation, multi-seed evaluation, and cross-platform benchmarking improved robustness, broader generalization still requires multi-season and multi-site campaigns with harmonized acquisition protocols and repeated external validation. \rev{Transferability to other regions should be framed at the level of the pipeline rather than the trained weights: the learned spatial gradients are specific to Lago do Serpa, so direct application of the fitted model to another lake is not expected to preserve accuracy. What transfers is the methodology --- feature design, augmentation policy, statistical protocol, and deployment stack --- which can retrain locally from a comparably small campaign in a new site. Cross-site fine-tuning and domain-adaptation strategies are planned to quantify this transfer cost explicitly.} Reproducibility is a practical strength of this pipeline: the modeling protocol, seed-based evaluation, and offline deployment measurements are explicit and portable, allowing direct replication in other low-connectivity contexts. Beyond predictive metrics, the Raspberry Pi deployment achieved sub-millisecond inference and the ESP32 validation confirmed autonomous offline execution under constrained resources, reinforcing that local inference is practical across heterogeneous edge profiles.
