\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented an energy-aware offline multimodal SNN pipeline for water-quality prediction in an Amazonian edge-monitoring context. The proposed approach demonstrated practical feasibility from training to deployment, including geospatial interpretation and cross-platform latency/energy benchmarking. The results indicate that neuromorphic inference is deployable under real hardware constraints, with measurable platform-specific trade-offs between throughput and energy per inference. While not always the best in absolute MAE, the SNN remains a robust option when temporal event-driven processing and edge compatibility are first-class requirements.

Threats to validity should be explicitly considered when interpreting these findings. The main internal-validity risk is the limited size of the original field dataset, which can increase split sensitivity and make small metric gaps unstable across partitions; this was mitigated through repeated-seed evaluation, conservative augmentation, and per-target reporting, but not fully eliminated. External validity is also bounded by hydrological diversity across Amazonian subregions, so broader regional claims require multi-site, multi-season campaigns with harmonized acquisition protocols. Future work includes broader field acquisition across seasonal regimes, external validation in additional freshwater systems \rev{and lakes}, and deeper co-optimization of encoding parameters and hardware execution modes for improved energy-performance balance. \rev{Planned methodological extensions include full k-fold cross-validation on the enlarged dataset, a systematic comparison of augmentation strategies beyond Gaussian perturbation, measurement of the energy consumption of Random Forest, LSTM, and MLP under the same idle/load protocol used for the SNN on identical devices, and enriched input modalities to address the dissolved-oxygen limitation identified in the per-target analysis.} Overall, this study demonstrates that neuromorphic models can support practical environmental monitoring in remote regions by enabling energy-aware edge intelligence for water-quality prediction in the Amazon.
