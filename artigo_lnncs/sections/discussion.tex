\section{Discussion}

Results show a clear trade-off between predictive accuracy and deployment constraints. Although Random Forest reached slightly lower MAE, the SNN remained competitive while offering event-driven temporal processing and direct alignment with neuromorphic edge deployment goals. This is relevant for low-power autonomous monitoring where model choice is not driven by accuracy alone. From a methodological standpoint, the multimodal setup proved valuable because it keeps physically related targets in a single inference pipeline and simplifies downstream decision logic in field applications. The observed error profile also indicates nonuniform target difficulty: pH and OD were more stable, while turbidity concentrated a larger share of uncertainty. This asymmetry is expected in heterogeneous freshwater environments and supports the use of weighted losses, repeated-seed reporting, and per-target diagnostics rather than relying on a single aggregate metric.

From the systems perspective, platform behavior is heterogeneous: Device B reached the highest throughput, Device C achieved the lowest incremental energy per inference, and ESP32 provided full offline feasibility at much lower processing rates. Therefore, deployment decisions should be scenario-dependent: high-rate analytics benefit from smartphone/SBC-class hardware, while sparse periodic sensing can still be addressed by microcontrollers. Energy interpretation should be made with explicit operational context. Incremental power ($\Delta P$) captures the additional cost of inference above platform idle baseline, but this baseline itself varies by device architecture, operating system behavior, and workload scheduling. Consequently, comparing only absolute watts may be misleading; energy per inference combined with throughput provides a more actionable criterion for selecting hardware according to sampling cadence and autonomy requirements. The environmental interpretation is consistent with the literature and regulatory practice, but generalization remains limited by dataset size. Augmentation and multi-seed evaluation reduced split sensitivity, yet broader field campaigns across seasons and additional water bodies are still required for stronger external validity. Another practical implication is that offline inference meaningfully improves deployment resilience in low-connectivity regions. Even when cloud links are unavailable, the proposed pipeline maintains local prediction, map generation, and relative-risk screening. This supports continuous operation in remote campaigns and reduces dependence on synchronous backhaul. Future extensions should include external calibration across multiple Amazonian lakes, adaptive retraining strategies, and systematic stress tests under longer autonomous missions.
