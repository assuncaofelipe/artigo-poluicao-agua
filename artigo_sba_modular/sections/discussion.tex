\section{Discussion}

Results show a clear trade-off between predictive accuracy and deployment constraints. In the multi-seed benchmark, Random Forest and LSTM reached lower average macro errors, while the SNN remained competitive and clearly more stable than MLP. Sign-test outcomes (SNN vs Random Forest) did not indicate statistically significant global superiority at $\alpha=0.05$, which reinforces that the SNN should be interpreted as a robust edge-oriented alternative rather than a universal best-accuracy model. From a methodological standpoint, the multimodal setup proved valuable because it keeps physically related targets in a single inference pipeline and simplifies downstream decision logic in field applications. The observed error profile also indicates nonuniform target difficulty: pH and OD were more stable, while turbidity concentrated a larger share of uncertainty. This asymmetry is expected in heterogeneous freshwater environments and supports repeated-seed reporting and per-target diagnostics rather than relying on a single aggregate metric.

From the systems perspective, platform behavior is heterogeneous: Device B reached the highest throughput, Device C achieved the lowest incremental energy per inference, and ESP32 provided full offline feasibility at much lower processing rates. Therefore, deployment decisions should be scenario-dependent: high-rate analytics benefit from smartphone/SBC-class hardware, while sparse periodic sensing can still be addressed by microcontrollers. Energy interpretation should be made with explicit operational context, since baseline platform power varies with architecture and operating-system behavior. For this reason, energy per inference combined with throughput is a more actionable criterion than absolute watts alone. The environmental interpretation is consistent with the literature and regulatory practice, but generalization remains limited by dataset size. Augmentation ablation showed a modest but consistent macro-MAE improvement (2.53\%) and turbidity gain, indicating that controlled synthetic expansion helps stabilize training in this low-sample regime. Even so, broader field campaigns are still required for stronger external validity. Offline inference remains a practical advantage in low-connectivity regions by preserving local prediction and screening without cloud dependence.

\subsection{Practical Deployment Guidance}
The combined results allow a simple deployment guideline for field teams. If the operational goal is dense temporal sampling, frequent map updates, or local dashboards with near-real-time interaction, smartphone or SBC-class execution is preferable because the throughput margin is orders of magnitude higher than ESP32 and keeps incremental energy per inference low in absolute terms. In contrast, if the objective is sparse periodic checks with strict hardware simplicity, ESP32 remains viable and operationally attractive due to robustness, low integration complexity, and full offline operation, despite higher mJ/inf values.

A second practical point is model-selection policy. In constrained field scenarios, selecting only by minimum MAE can be suboptimal. A better criterion is a multi-objective envelope: acceptable prediction error, stable seed behavior, platform throughput, and incremental energy per inference under realistic idle/load transitions. Under this lens, SNN becomes a robust compromise model for edge deployment workflows, especially when local inference continuity and resilience to connectivity outages are first-class requirements. This decision framework can be reused in future campaigns and extended with additional constraints such as battery budget, expected daily inference count, and telemetry upload cadence.

\subsection{Error Interpretation and Validity}
The predicted-vs-true plots indicate a clear target-dependent error profile: pH and OD follow the identity trend with lower dispersion, while turbidity exhibits wider spread. This behavior is coherent with freshwater heterogeneity and reinforces that turbidity should be interpreted with broader confidence margins in operational use. From an application perspective, this asymmetry supports a layered decision strategy: treat pH and OD as stronger short-term screening signals, and use turbidity primarily to flag candidate zones for additional sampling rather than as a standalone trigger. This reduces false confidence in high-variance regions while preserving actionable monitoring cadence.

Threats to validity remain centered on sample size and regional representativeness. Although augmentation, multi-seed evaluation, and cross-platform benchmarking improved robustness, broader generalization still requires multi-season and multi-site campaigns with harmonized acquisition protocols and repeated external validation. Reproducibility is a practical strength of this pipeline: the modeling protocol, seed-based evaluation, and offline deployment measurements are all explicit and portable. This allows direct replication in other low-connectivity contexts and enables incremental extension with new sensors, alternative models, or stricter energy constraints.

The results also indicate that the neuromorphic approach is technically viable for predicting physicochemical variables in this Amazonian context after systematic tuning and early-stopping regularization. In the leakage-free multimodal configuration, prediction difficulty increases as expected, especially for turbidity, which makes the evaluation more realistic for field operation. In the 10-seed benchmark, Random Forest and LSTM obtained lower global mean errors, while SNN remained competitive and substantially more robust than MLP. This pattern is consistent with the sign-test outcomes and supports SNN as an edge-oriented alternative even when it is not the absolute best in macro error.

Beyond predictive metrics, the Raspberry Pi deployment achieved sub-millisecond inference and the ESP32 validation confirmed autonomous offline execution under constrained resources. Together, these results reinforce that local inference is practical across heterogeneous edge profiles, from higher-throughput nodes to low-rate microcontroller scenarios. Another relevant design point is temporal encoding through rate coding: increasing $T$ can improve temporal representation, but also raises computational cost and energy demand. Therefore, in real deployments, $T$ should be calibrated jointly with sampling cadence, battery budget, and platform throughput constraints.
